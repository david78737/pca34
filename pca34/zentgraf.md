# What Snacking in Star Trek Teaches Us About Building with Generative AI
**Dan Zentgraf** · PCA34 · April 18, 2026

## Synopsis
Zentgraf uses the lens of Star Trek's food synthesis technology to explore the precision, bias, and constraint challenges inherent in building generative AI products. The analogy reveals how human intent is imprecise, how model biases shape outputs in unexpected ways, and how resource constraints force product tradeoffs that aren't always visible to users. For product managers building AI features, this is a conceptual framework disguised as a delightful talk.

## Key Takeaways
- Human intent is fundamentally imprecise — AI products must be designed to handle the gap between what users say and what they mean
- Model biases manifest in real product outputs in ways that parallel how a replicator might default to cultural assumptions about food
- Resource constraints in AI systems create product design tradeoffs that need to be surfaced and managed, not hidden

## Who Should Read This
Product managers and designers building generative AI features who want a memorable, conceptually rich framework for thinking about intent, bias, and constraint in their systems.

## The Brief
The next time you define an AI feature's scope, ask the Star Trek question: what happens when the user's request is technically fulfilled but completely wrong — and design for that gap.
